# U.S. Series Seed Rules

The U.S. knowledge layer should start with high-use series and expand incrementally.

## Initial examples
- U.S. Dime — Barber (1892–1916)
- U.S. Dime — Mercury (1916–1945)
- U.S. Dime — Roosevelt (1946–present)
- U.S. Dollar — Morgan (1878–1921)
- U.S. Dollar — Peace (1921–1935)
- U.S. Half Dollar — Walking Liberty (1916–1947)
- U.S. Half Dollar — Franklin (1948–1963)
- U.S. Half Dollar — Kennedy (1964–present)
- U.S. Quarter — State Quarters (1999–2008)
- U.S. Quarter — America the Beautiful (2010–2021)
- U.S. Quarter — American Women Quarters (2022–2025)

These are seed knowledge only. They must not be treated as an exhaustive list, and future additions must not require changing the item data model.

## Override and uncertainty
Every seed rule produces a suggestion. The user can accept, edit, or override it. If identifying information conflicts or is incomplete, the system should show an uncertainty state rather than silently assigning a series.

## Scope
The architecture remains worldwide. U.S. rules are prioritized for initial usefulness, not treated as the only supported country or issuer.
