# Professional Visual Design Principles

## Goal
The catalog should feel polished, trustworthy, and appropriate for serious collectors while still having a friendly personality through its original mascots.

## Design balance
- Mascots are accents and guides, not the visual identity of every screen.
- Primary catalog screens prioritize coin photos, identification, organization, and readability.
- Decorative elements should use restraint, whitespace, consistent typography, and a coherent visual hierarchy.
- Professional/Dealer Mode should feel especially clean and information-dense without becoming cluttered.
- The fun personality should come from character moments, onboarding, and optional screensaver behavior rather than filling every screen with cartoons.

## Mascot placement
- Keep mascots near onboarding/help moments, empty states, optional tips, and screensaver scenes.
- Do not place a mascot beside every button or field.
- Never allow mascots to cover coin images, values, important controls, warnings, or search results.
- Mascot animations should be short and purposeful when they appear in ordinary workflows.

## Typography and controls
- Use a restrained type hierarchy with large, readable primary information.
- Use consistent button shapes, spacing, and interaction states.
- Avoid novelty fonts for essential catalog information.
- Keep important coin data visually more prominent than decorative content.

## Color and imagery
- Use a limited, cohesive palette rather than bright colors competing for attention.
- Coin photography should remain the visual focus of collection records.
- Decorative imagery should complement rather than imitate professional grading/reference interfaces.

## Product personality
The product can be described internally as "classy, simple, and fun." It should be approachable to a young collector without looking like a children's game and comfortable for an older or professional collector to use every day.

## Accessibility
Professional appearance must never come at the expense of readability, contrast, tap-target size, reduced-motion support, or screen-reader semantics.
