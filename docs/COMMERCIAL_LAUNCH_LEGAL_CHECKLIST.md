# Commercial Launch Legal Checklist

## Purpose
Keep legal work organized for the final pre-launch review without making legal review a blocker for ordinary development and testing.

## During development
- Build original mascots, artwork, animations, icons, sounds, and UI copy.
- Keep third-party integrations behind permitted APIs/links and document their terms.
- Avoid copying competitor artwork, source code, distinctive wording, or branding.
- Keep privacy/security decisions documented as features are implemented.
- Maintain a simple record of major original product decisions and creation dates.

## Before public commercial launch
Have qualified counsel review the finished product and documents, including:
- Terms of Service.
- Privacy Policy.
- Data-loss, security, and service-availability language.
- Subscription, renewal, cancellation, refund, and downgrade terms.
- User-generated content and uploaded-photo provisions.
- Account deletion and data-export rules.
- Support and collection-recovery procedures.
- Children's/age-related requirements if the final product is marketed to or knowingly used by children.
- Third-party API/data licenses and attribution requirements.
- Trademark/brand and mascot-name clearance.
- Copyright ownership/assignment for commissioned artwork and code where applicable.
- Any patentability questions the owner wants evaluated.

## Patent strategy
The product does not require a patent in order to be built or launched. Do not assume a patent is necessary. If the owner later believes a genuinely novel technical invention is worth protecting, obtain patent counsel before making public disclosures that could affect filing options. This project will not claim that a feature is patentable without professional review.

## Release gate
The app should not be described as legally reviewed or legally guaranteed until the owner has actually obtained the professional review. Legal documents should be finalized before commercial launch rather than copied from another product.

## Product-development rule
Legal review is a launch-stage gate, not a reason to stop ordinary prototyping, usability testing, or architecture work. The project should continue to use conservative originality and third-party-content practices throughout development.

This checklist is planning guidance, not legal advice.
