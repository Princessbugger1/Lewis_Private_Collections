# American Coinage Identification Rules

U.S. coin identification should favor predictable, editable date/type rules while allowing exceptions and user corrections.

- The catalog should use denomination, year, mint, design/type, and other applicable attributes to suggest a U.S. series.
- Series suggestions must remain editable and must not overwrite a confirmed user classification without approval.
- Date ranges should support major U.S. design/type transitions where appropriate.
- Examples include Barber, Mercury, and Roosevelt dimes; the app may suggest the appropriate type from the relevant date and denomination rules.
- U.S. quarter programs and commemoratives may have specialized series/subviews, including user-added sets such as America the Beautiful quarters.
- Key-date, mint, variety, proof, business-strike, and commemorative distinctions should remain separate fields or classifications when applicable rather than being hidden inside a display name.
- Ancient, colonial, territorial, pattern, token, medal, and other nonstandard items must not be forced into ordinary U.S. circulation-coin rules.
- If date or type information is missing or ambiguous, the record remains editable and may be marked Needs Review rather than receiving a guessed classification.
- New U.S. series and corrections to date/type rules should be data-driven so the catalog can expand without requiring every user to replace existing records.
